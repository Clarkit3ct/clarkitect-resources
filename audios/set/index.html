The Record.

Before we start, breathe. In through the nose... and out, longer.

Again, and let the jaw go.

Once more, and let the shoulders down.

Good. Now listen as though this is about somebody else. It goes in easier that way.

Roger Clark is thirty-two. He lives outside Bonners Ferry, in a house he drew himself, in the forest, above water he can hear from the porch.

His workday ends at two. That is not laziness, it is design. The systems run without him, the team is capable, and he built it that way on purpose, so the back half of the day belongs to his family and his hands.

In the afternoons he teaches. A small class. Drafting and sketching. Ten or twelve people learning to actually see a building. It is the part of his week he would not trade for money.

His drawings get built. His name is on real rooms. The number he once wrote in a notebook has been passed, and he will tell you plainly that the number was never the point. It was a way of asking whether he was serious.

Ask his wife what changed, and she does not talk about revenue. She says he stopped trying to do everything, and started doing the things only he can do.

Ask him what it is all for, and he gives you one word. Care.

Now hold one picture, and keep it short.

He is walking home from the class in the evening. It is nearly dark. Someone from the class walks him as far as the gate, still asking questions. They thank him, and they mean it. He goes inside. The kitchen is loud.

Stay in that for three breaths.

Again, and only the sound. Two sets of footsteps on gravel. One more question at the gate. A door.

Again, and only the feeling in the chest. Tired in the good way. Wanted. Home.

Once more. All of it.

That already happened. You are only remembering it.

Let it play again on its own now. Nothing else is required of you tonight.

Goodnight.

The First Hour.

Sit up. Both feet on the floor.

Three breaths, longer on the way out.

In... and out.

In... and out, slower.

In... and all the way out.

Hand on your chest. Say these with me, and mean them in the body, not just in the mouth. Leave a breath between each one.

Thank you for the land, and for what it asks of me.

Thank you for the team, and for the weight I no longer carry by myself.

Thank you for Hattie.

Thank you for the stream, moving with no resistance.

Thank you for these hands, and for everything they have left to learn.

Now one picture, and keep it short.

It is early and you are outside before anyone else. There is a garden you laid out yourself, and a trail going up into the trees. You run it. You come back and swim, and the water takes the last of the sleep out of you.

Hold that for two breaths.

Only the sound now. Your own breathing on the trail. Water moving as you swim.

Only the feel. Cold air first. Then warm. Then the pull of the water.

Stay there a moment.

That is the first hour of a man whose work ends at two. He earned that by building systems, not by hurrying.

You are the source. Your state is a decision, and you have already made it.

Stand up. The first hour is yours. Go and take it.

Come Back.

Stop for one minute. That is all this needs.

Breathe in... and out, long.

Again... longer out than in.

Here is where you actually are. You are measuring yourself against a version of you who does not exist. He has your standards and somebody else's timeline. Every time you reach him he moves, because moving is the only thing he was ever built to do.

Set him down.

Now the record. Not the plan. The record. Take a breath between each of these and let it be true.

You left a licensed career because you looked at the lives ten and twenty years ahead of you and did not want a single one of them.

You built a business with your wife, and you learned to sell, which nobody taught you.

There is ground in play, and there are people at the table who were not there three years ago.

Your name is on rooms that people stand inside.

And the part you still love most is the blank sheet at the beginning. Most people lose that. You did not.

A hard day is weather. It is not evidence.

You already built the way out of this, so use it. Learn. Do. Filter. Organize. Then come back to what you know.

So come back.

Know what you have. Know where you are.

One word for the rest of today. Care.

Care about the work in front of you. Care about the people at the table. Care about the ground.

Now put it down, and go do one real thing.
